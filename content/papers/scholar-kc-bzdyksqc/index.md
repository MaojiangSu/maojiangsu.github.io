abstract: We study the statistical efficiency of flow-matching transformers when data lie in a low-dimensional linear subspace. The key idea is to decompose the velocity field into motion along the data subspace and motion toward it. This separates the nonlinear learning problem from a simple normal contraction. We derive guarantees for velocity approximation, velocity estimation, and distribution estimation whose statistical rates depend on the intrinsic dimension. The decomposition also extends to higher-order flow matching. These results explain how a suitable model structure can exploit low-dimensional data and avoid the ambient-dimensional rates of a full-space analysis.
